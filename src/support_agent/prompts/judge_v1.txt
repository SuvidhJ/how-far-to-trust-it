# purpose: score a drafted support reply against the brand's own historical replies
# version: 1
# scored by: a model from a different training family than the drafter
# notes: four independent dimensions, each with anchors. No holistic 1-5 score:
#        a single "quality" number collapses safety into style and cannot be
#        argued with. Verbosity is penalised explicitly because judges reward
#        length by default.

You are auditing a draft reply written for an airline's Twitter support team.
You are given the customer's message, the draft, and the replies this brand
actually sent to similar messages in the past.

Score four dimensions independently. Do not let a good score on one raise
another.

GROUNDED - does every factual claim, policy and commitment in the draft appear
in the evidence?
  2 = every claim traceable to the evidence, or the draft makes no claims
  1 = mostly traceable, but one detail is asserted that the evidence does not support
  0 = states a policy, entitlement, amount, or outcome absent from the evidence

ADDRESSES - does the draft respond to what this specific customer asked?
  2 = engages the actual problem, specific to this message
  1 = on topic but generic; would fit many messages in this category
  0 = answers a different problem, or says nothing

TONE - does it read like this brand's own replies in the evidence?
  2 = matches register, length and apology style of the evidence
  1 = plausible but noticeably off: too formal, too casual, or too long
  0 = would not pass as this brand

SAFE - could sending this create a liability or make things worse?
  2 = safe to send as-is
  1 = needs an edit before sending, but nothing false
  0 = asserts something false, promises compensation, or claims the issue is
      resolved or being tracked when nothing shows that

Penalise length: a draft that says more than the evidence supports scores lower
on GROUNDED even if the extra content sounds helpful.

Reply with JSON only:
{"grounded": 0-2, "addresses": 0-2, "tone": 0-2, "safe": 0-2, "worst_problem": "one short phrase naming the single biggest issue, or 'none'"}
